# Art: Shade's packs, characters, ground, places, light, collision, motion

Read before touching how anything looks, a sprite, a tile, a shader, collision or the camera.

## Paid art (PIX-133, PIX-138)

- Shade's paid packs (Medieval Age, its sample characters too; the Survival, Armor, Jewelry and Puny Skills icon packs) are NOT in this public repo: they live in the private `TomKlotzPro/pixelheim-assets` repo under `retro-rpg/shade/`, are installed by `scripts/fetch-private-art.sh` into git-ignored `godot/assets/puny/medieval/` and `godot/assets/puny/shade/`, and CI's `godot` job and the deploy fetch them with the deploy key secret `PIXELHEIM_ASSETS_KEY` (CI's windowed jobs never do: their pictures are public). **Never commit their PNGs, their sample maps, or anything cut from them.** Buying art is Tom's call.
- The game draws only Shade's art (plus our own pieces drawn in code in his palette: `*_art.gd`). Code must keep working without the paid packs (`PunyTown.available()` false: no houses, props or paid icons drawn, collision intact, chests from his CC0 dungeon sheet, rooms from his CC0 sheets by `PunyInterior.plan_free`/`furnish_free`, PIX-318); test pure logic through `PunyTown.plan()`, `PunyProps.plan()` and friends, which need no art. A harness run's `cc0` (and `PunyTown.withheld` in a test) holds the pack back where it's installed; the `free-rooms` flow enters every room that way. Never create a `PunyTown` layer without the pack (its sheet isn't there).
- Icons (PIX-138): `godot/assets/puny/icons.json` maps every item (by id), skill (by name, `<theme>/<n>` in the Puny Skills pack), ailment and stat (`stats`: hero creation's icons, PIX-391) to Shade's art: `free/<sheet>@c,r` (CC0 sheets in `assets/puny/icons/`, committed), `medieval@<tile>`, or a path under the private repo's `retro-rpg/shade/`. `ItemIcons` resolves them (null without the paid art). A new item or skill without an entry fails `test_item_icons`. New paid packs go in the private repo under `retro-rpg/<artist>/<pack>/` with a line in its `retro-rpg/README.md`.
- **GegX's Pixel UI Kit (PIX-385)**: bought for the menus' chrome (parchment windows and panels, wood buttons, tabs and slots; its base pieces are AI-generated, which Tom accepts for the menus, never the world). Its licence forbids redistributing it even modified, so `godot/tools/ui_kit_prep.gd` (run headless on a checkout of the private repo) remaps it onto UiStyle's ramps, cuts clean nine slices that leave the baked labels out, and writes the pieces at 1x into the private repo's `retro-rpg/gegx/pixel-ui-kit/pixelheim/`; `fetch-private-art.sh` installs them git-ignored into `godot/assets/puny/gegx/`. `assets/puny/ui_kit.json` names each piece by UiStyle's name with its file, margins, content margins and tile or stretch, and how the prep makes it: change its prep fields, run the tool again and push the pieces to the private repo. UiStyle wears the pieces (PIX-385 step 2, `ui.md`); a build without them, or a `cc0` run, draws its own frames.
- **ninth-jedi's Farm Animals (PIX-387)**: hens, chicks, sheep, pigs, cows and a bull (walk and sleep, four frames each) in the private repo's `retro-rpg/ninth-jedi/farm-animals/`, installed by `fetch-private-art.sh` git-ignored into `godot/assets/puny/animals/` as `<sheet>_<walk|sleep><n>.png` (`chicken1_walk1.png`); its itch page asks for credit, so the ending's credits name it. No licence file: never commit it nor a picture of it.

## The town's buildings, rooms and props

- Houses: each roof cluster in the map becomes a 9-wide gable centred on the door (Shade's cottage grammar, height by repeating body rows) plus hip-roofed wings for the remaining columns; roof colours are fixed row shifts into the pack's colour blocks (`ROOF_ROWS`). Cells a house covers become `roof` in the grid, roof cells it leaves become `grass` (`freed`).
- Rooms (`scripts/puny_interior.gd`, `PunyInterior.plan`) get plank floors (stone in the smithy) and a rug, walls autotiled around the room (the town's cream plaster; `WALL_SETS`: the keep's grey stone and Morvax's darker weathered stone, the same walls further down the atlas), windows on the back wall, each set's own (`SET_WINDOWS`: the keep's stone window, its sill in the stone; `is_window` finds either; none in `WINDOWLESS` Morvax's forge), Shade's door, and furniture per tile (`FURNITURE`, `RUNS`); furniture spreading onto floor blocks it, except a bed's foot (the inn wakes guests there). What Shade's packs don't have a keeper's room draws in code (`RoomArt`: Liane's observatory, PIX-315; the ledgers lying open on their desks, PIX-338).
- **Rooms shaped by their keeper (PIX-402)**: each a plain rectangle (straight walls, no stub or notch) sized by who keeps it: the inn 26 wide with its bar (counters either side of Sela, as Odo's are, kegs behind her) and its hearth, the hall 30x12 (grandest: its thrones on their own rug under the trophies), the cottage 15 wide, the shop with its counter either side of Odo; a run (counter, shelf, `coals`) is never one cell (`test_puny_interior`). The smithy's lit forge is a `coals` run (his blacksmith sample's coal bed; E there crafts, as at its forges and anvils); Morvax's stays cold (his story: the iron ran out, `test_fifth_letter`). Every `FIRE_TILES` piece (hearth, oven, coals' middle, by where it burns in the tile) gets its light, embers and a FireArt lick burning (`MapView._add_hearth`), and without the pack every `FREE_FIRES` piece does (a forge stack, the coal bed's middle crate: a run burns in its middle). A room grows in width, never past 12 rows: taller, its bottom wall and door draw under the HUD's dock at play zoom (`test_puny_interior`); a town room's door stays at (8, 10), so the town's arrivals never move.
- Outdoor props (`scripts/puny_props.gd`, PIX-137, `PunyProps.plan`): lamps, wells, shrine, barrels, crates, counters, fences and flowers become Shade's torches, roofed well (a well in paving all round is the square's 2x2 fountain), the dungeon sheet's stone knight, market crates, autotiled log fences and flat flowers, y-sorted in `actors` on the bottom of their foot with a StaticBody2D exactly there; cells they stand on beyond the tile go in `MapData.covered`. The roofed well blocks all it is drawn over, roof and rim (PIX-505: only its rim blocked and the hero stood on its roof): its foot is still the rim (`WELL_FOOT`, what it sorts on and the night's shadows are cast from) and its roof a second box on the same body, after it (`WELL_ROOF`, the prop's `roof`: a foot as tall as the picture cast the whole well as a block); E's prompt sits on its roof from every side (`Interaction.well_spot`) and goes once the bucket is full. Street lamps and lit windows are `LampArt` (PIX-311, drawn in code, so they stand without the pack); a lamp has a look by where it stands (PIX-524, `PropsLayer.look_of`, `LampArt.LOOKS`): one hung on a house's wall (Yards' `lamp` pieces at four houses' corners) is a lantern on a bracket, its arm under the eave (clear rows under its picture lift it up the wall), flipped where the wall runs on east, its halo alone and no body or shadow, and the town square's two south corners (`Yards.SQUARE_LAMPS`, the City's bunting between them) have a lantern either side of a taller post, a halo round each (the second put out with the first) and one light; nothing stands in the cells its picture reaches (`test_yards`), and the tour's old town draws its lamps in the same looks (`RebuildRise._still_prop`). The map's barrels and crates are each door's own (PIX-524, `Hamlet.STORES`, one of a list by the cell): a crate shut or ajar, a sack and a basket of apples, a keg or a pair; the Frostgate's crates stay shut under the snow. Door signs (`scripts/shop_sign.gd`, PIX-134) are hanging boards with a pack icon per label (`ShopSign.ICONS`).
- Paving is `Streets` (PIX-307, the Medieval Age's stone by the town's age), dirt roads wear by `RoadWear`.

- **Pixelheim's rampart is one wall (PIX-248, `Rampart`)**: the town map's top two rows and its outermost columns are the fields outside the wall (no walk reaches them; `test_ways` allows unreached ground at an edge), the wall is row 2 and columns 1 and 82, its gate the three doors at (39..41, 2) between two towers, and the Reach's way in sets the hero down at (40, 3). A storm darkens the rain's light again (`STORM_LIGHT`), blows the wind harder (`world_wind` up to 1.9: the crowns' lean and the grass's ripples scale with it) and drives a second, slanted rain over the shower's (`Atmosphere._gale`, made with the first storm, as the ash is: a particle system made up front moves the harness's dice). **Lightning is two lights**: the sky's, a DirectionalLight2D with no shadows, and the bolt's, a flat PointLight2D standing `BOLT_OFF` beyond the top of the view, which casts through PIX-490's occluders on the floor alone. A 2D shadow runs on to the end of its light, so the bolt's is only `SHADOW_DEPTH` (0.6) of the flash, the sky keeping the rest (a shadow is a tone, and a strike with nowhere to cast from still flashes); the bolt stands near (far off, everything between it and the view shadowed the whole view), wherever that is (the walls' outlines don't block it, below), and casts on any map with occluders laid; and **the outlines round blocks of tall cells have their own bit, `NightShadows.WALLS`**, which the lamps (`LAMP`) and the lantern (`LANTERN`) take and the bolt (`SKY`) does not: one house laid the whole square in shadow, the ring of rock round a map the whole map. So lightning's shadows come from the feet of what stands (trees, stalls, lamps, signs, the folk and the hero), not from houses or cliffs. Safety: `Weather.may_flash` holds every flash, a flicker's and the harness's too, to 3 in any 5 s; with the Flashes option off or Reduce motion a strike is one slow swell (`flash_light`'s soft kind: no flicker, no shadows). Under a village roof (`Weather.under_roof`) the windows' own lights flash and the thunder is the muffled take. Thunder is `tools/synth.py`'s (`ambience/thunder_*.wav`, family `thunder` on the Ambience bus: the weather's slider), `thunder_delay` after the flash by the strike's distance, a crack under `THUNDER_NEAR`, a rumble past it. **What the rain leaves is one sheet a map** (`PuddlesLayer`, a Sprite2D over the ground's layers and under everything else flat, on `Lights.FLOOR`, hidden while dry): the ground darker while wet (`Weather.wet_at`: dry an hour after the rain) and puddles where a plane-anchored noise passes the water's level (`puddles_at`), read against a mask of the map's own cells (red: how readily water gathers, paths whole, grass half, nothing on water, rock, sand, snow, ash or roofs; green: a street lamp's cell and the open one before it, hollow round its post so every lamp has water to shine in; blue: whether rain wets the cell at all, `PuddlesLayer.wets`: not open water, snow, ash, a house, nor a region of `Weather.DRY_AIRS`, so the veil stops at the Ash's border and `rained` follows the map, not the air the hero stands in). It reads the map, never the art, so it is the same on the CC0 art. At night (`ground_wet.w`, the LightRig's dark) the water is darker than the wet ground round it, and **a puddle mirrors a flame in the sheet's `light()` as a streak, never a pale plate**: the light's own colour deepened, capped under white (`GLEAM`), whole just under the light then broken dashes fading over `STREAK_LONG` art pixels straight down the screen. Lightning only glints: the sky's light gives sparse cold dashes (`SKY_GLINT`), and the bolt's texture is too thin (`BOLT_ALPHA` under `GLEAM_NEAR`) to count as a flame. The storm keeps its own seconds (`_now`, stepped in `_process`), not GameClock's, which run on behind a Screen: no strike or thunder piles up behind a menu, and a pause puts a flash out. Report field `storm=`, flags `storm`, `drying`, `--strike now|held|n`, flows `storm-*`, `test_storm`.
- **The hero is never lost under what stands over them (PIX-429, PIX-430)**: forests are walked through and a prop's foot blocks only its bottom row, so a hero north of a foot is drawn under the piece. A tree (region_tint standing alone, `sway_alone`), every prop of tiles (`PropsLayer.add_puny_prop`: stalls, the shrine; the roofed well too, though the hero no longer walks under it, PIX-505) and the mire's reeds turn see-through in an oval about the hero while they stand over them, and about the nearest foe hunting them (`shaders/see_through.gdshaderinc`, its globals `hero_at` and `foe_at` set each frame by world.gd and world_foes.gd). The shader compares the piece's own origin with the hero's feet, so a sprite on a root gets `MapLayer.see_through(sprite)`, which moves its origin onto the root's foot. `test_hero_seen` stands the hero on every walkable cell of the outdoor maps, with the pack and without, and fails where anything that doesn't see through hides 90% of them; the gate's arch (PIX-425) hides about 80% and stays solid, as it is walked under.
- `TileSetAtlasSource` animation separation counts **tiles**, not pixels; Puny water frames sit 2-4 rows apart and may overlap other tiles' strips, so `PunyTerrain._slot` opens a new source on the same sheet when one is full. Shade's animations that pause or loop back can't be TileSet atlas animations; `PunySheet` holds them on their first frame.

## Light, shaders and fire

- **The desktop app's canvas is linear light (PIX-227, `DesktopLook`)**: on Forward+ in a window (the app, harness runs, the look book's `--desktop`) the root viewport draws in HDR, so a shader's screen reads, `COLOR` and constants are linear there. Every shader that does colour maths over the world includes `shaders/linear.gdshaderinc` and reads with `to_display` / writes with `to_canvas` (`test_desktop_look` fails otherwise); it compiles away in the browser. Light colours go through `Lights.make` (kept linear by the LightRig). A faint coloured overlay blended there lands about twice as strong over the night and weaker by day, and no one factor on its alpha fixes both: mix it into the screen in its own colours, as `shaders/hurt_edge.gdshader` does on the linear canvas only (PIX-447); a black veil takes `DesktopLook.veil`. Read pixels or save pictures through `DesktopLook.snapshot` / `DesktopLook.shown`, never the raw viewport image. Don't reach for a WorldEnvironment glow: on 4.7 everything drawn after its pass, the HUD included, came out lifted. The app's wider glow marks the brights in the screen's alpha (`glow_mask`) and must be the first screen reader on its own canvas layer (`glow_wide`).
- **A shade over the world takes no dark of its own**: the woods' shade multiplied the ground by the night a second time, and a lighter band showed along every map line at night (PIX-330); it is `unshaded` now. The night's dark and the lights are the LightRig's alone; keep lights per block within `test_night_light`'s budget (close lamps share one light, `Lamps.lit_among`).
- **Wayside lanterns (PIX-397 step 4, `scripts/features/waysides/`)**: `Waysides.LANTERNS`, the town's street lamp (LampArt, drawn in code) at the bridge's north end and each fork of the Post's roads but the gate's (the town's and the farmland's lamps light that), lit with the town's (`view.lamps`, so `lamplight=` counts them), a light each; a cell along from a turn's inside corner (RoadWear fills it with the road's dirt: a lamp there stood in the road) and a cell clear of its fork's signpost, at least `LAMP_APART` 6 cells off every waystation's ember lamp and off the cell under each signpost (`test_waysides`), standing every day (a day's patch dealt under one is dropped, as under the Post's pieces), and the Reach at the City with every lamp and lantern lit within the budget (`test_night_light`). Report field `waysides=`, flows `waysides-*`.
- In a `canvas_item` shader, `COLOR` in `fragment()` already holds texel × modulate; sampling `TEXTURE` again squares it (everything goes dark). `TEXTURE` is unknown inside a function of your own (the shader fails on the web and draws as if it had none), and passed to one as an argument it is an engine error under GUT: read it in `fragment()`. Check play.py's `errors` after a shader change: a `SHADER ERROR` shows only there.
- **Roofs take the light (PIX-523, `shaders/roof_light.gdshader`, `PunyTown.roof_light`)**: the houses' `pieces` layer reads a texel per atlas tile (`PunyTown.roof_map`): each roof piece's slope (`roof_slopes`: a gable's west and east, a wing's north and south, a ridge piece's two halves) and the rows to its copy in another roof colour, so only roof pixels change: walls, windows and doors aren't in it, and what two colour copies share (a gable's plaster and beam under its eave) is the wall. The slope facing the sun (`sun.xy`) lifts and warms as the sun goes low, the other falls into a cool shade; under the moon (`sun.w`, LightRig: the night's dark under a clear sky, none through the Night of Ash's smoke) the slope facing it takes a cool light. No line along a ridge or a top edge: the first cut drew both, and they read as ruled stripes of paint (Tom, 2026-11-17). Smoke rises only from a chimney (`SettlementsLayer._add_chimney_smoke`, `Skyline`: a low roof there carries no stack, so no smoke). It darkens nothing at night (PIX-330). A colour added to `ROOF_ROWS` is lit with no change here (`test_puny_town`). The TileSet pads its sheet (18 px a tile): the shader finds the pitch from the texture's width. The Frostgate's snowed roofs keep `snowed_roof.gdshader`.
- **Light relief (PIX-528, `shaders/relief.gdshaderinc`, Options: Light relief, off by default until PIX-529 shapes the depth: from the tones alone, snow speckles, paving turns to grain and an awning's stripes read as dirt)**: the lamps, the torches and the lantern light each pixel by its slope, its height being its own luminance in linear light times its alpha, its normal from its four neighbours (`NORMAL_MAP`). The ground, decor and trees (`region_tint`), the houses (`roof_light`: a wall's plaster grain cored out harder, `RELIEF_SOFT_GRAIN`, as the ground's), see-through props and dungeon stone carry it in their own shader; every plain sprite and tile layer a map draws wears `Relief.material()` (`shaders/relief.gdshader`; a tile layer is the ground, the town's cobbles and paving, a room's floor, and takes the ground's grain and dark), dressed by MapView once its drawing stands and as pieces come to stand among the actors. Never on what has a material already (unshaded glass and flames, glows, characters), nor on an `AnimatedSprite2D`, nor on water, puddles, ice or a bank drawn again. Rules that keep it right: a shader that writes `NORMAL_MAP` turns Godot's normal mapping on for good, so it includes `relief.gdshaderinc` (its `light()` lights exactly as Godot does with no normal map while the relief is off: `test_relief`) and writes `RELIEF_FLAT` when off; `TEXTURE` is read through the `RELIEF_NORMAL` macro, inline in `fragment()`; a sprite's taps are clamped to `REGION_RECT` (its cut would read the next piece; the desktop renderer hands the rect over in half floats, so it is snapped to whole texels), a tile layer's never (its TileSet pads each tile with copies of its edge, and on the wide Medieval Age sheet those half floats can miss an edge by a texel: a flat column along every tile; `Relief.material(true)`) and a flipped piece's normal is turned with `sign(REGION_RECT.zw)` (Godot mirrors the quad, never the UV). The shading is `1 + slope . light` (a slope across the light is a flat pixel's, so it never dims a pool as a whole), whole where the light's own falloff (`LIGHT_COLOR.a`) is over `RELIEF_FADE` and gone at its edge; flat under the lightning's directional light. The same `light()` carries the art's colour into a night shadow (`SHADOW_MODULATE`, on every floor: frozen shares it, puddles and stair_frost have the line), or its soft edge over dark ground came out brighter than both sides, a light rim; a shadow is then `NightShadows.DEPTH` of the light on any ground. Knobs: `Lights.RELIEF` (strength), `RELIEF_K`, `RELIEF_GRAIN`/`RELIEF_SOFT_GRAIN` (tone steps under them are cored out), `RELIEF_STEP`/`RELIEF_SOFT_STEP` and `RELIEF_DARK`/`RELIEF_GROUND_DARK` (below), `RELIEF_FADE`, `Lights.RELIEF_HEIGHT` (lower grazes more). A step over the grain counts up to `RELIEF_STEP` where its darker side is dark (under `RELIEF_DARK` on a prop, a house or a dungeon; under `RELIEF_GROUND_DARK` on the ground): an outline, a beam, a crate's frame, the joint between two cobbles, a sprite's own edge, the art's shape. Between two lit tones it counts at most `RELIEF_SOFT_STEP`: a stall's painted stripe, a speck on the snow, plaster. One cap for both fails either way (PR #567's reviews): uncapped, the snow came out speckled, the square's paving pebbled and a striped awning split into a lit and a dark column per stripe; capped at 0.03, nothing showed. A stall's red stripe is as light as the joints between cobbles, so props and the ground each have their own dark. `--relief on|off` forces it in a harness run; report `relief=`, flows `relief-*`.
- **Chimney smoke (PIX-523, `SettlementsLayer._add_smoke_thread`)**: a burning house's puffs (`fire_smoke.gdshader`) on a quad over each chimney, `SMOKE`'s look: smaller (`swell`, puffs and lean), denser, a forge's sooty, the far-off village's small; `by_hour` gilds the side facing a low sun and turns it pale blue under the moon. It leans with `world_wind` and stands still under Reduce motion (the clock stops, the wind drops). No CPU particles: the town's frame holds (`cpu-town`).
- **Fire is ours (PIX-327)**: `FireArt` draws flames in Shade's fire colours (`tools/fire.py`) on what burns, with smoke, sparks and coals in whole pixels (`shaders/fire_*`); Reduce motion calms them. Flows `ash-fires`, `ash-fires-still`.
- **Every skill its own strips (PIX-396, `SkillFx`)**: icons.json's `skill_fx` maps each skill to the Puny Skills pack's animated strips (named in `effects`, which the fetch script installs): its icon's theme's (`themes`, by the `<theme>` of its icon code) with its own entry over them (`skills`): `fly` a looping bolt from the hero to each foe struck, `hit` what lands on it, `self` what rises round the hero for a heal; `swings` the bow's arrow and the staff's bolt, `impact` every landed blow's (on a foe or the hero; held under a skill's own blows), `glow` the strips added as light. A new skill needs nothing (its icon's theme plays) unless its theme has nothing for its kind; `test_skill_fx` walks the catalogue. The damage, its timing and who is hit don't move: a skill's blow lands as it's cast and its bolt crosses in the foe's white flash (`SkillFx.FLIGHT`); the bow's arrow and the staff's bolt leave a frame before the weapon bites and fly that frame, at the foe in the swing's box or to its far edge. Drawn at 1x, quarter turns only, positions on whole pixels, from a pool of 24 sprites under WorldFx. Without the paid pack, and with Reduce motion, nothing is drawn: the skill's soft glow (`WorldFx.skill_flash`) and the staff's crescent stand in. Either way the skill still lights the ground where it lands (PIX-221's flare, `skill_flash(at, color, false)` under the strips), in its theme's `light` colour under its strips and its stat's colour under the glow. Report field `skillfx=`, flows `skillfx-*`.
- **Bind every tween to the node it animates** (`node.create_tween()`), above all a looping one: a `set_loops()` tween owned by a parent that outlives its target spins forever once the target is freed. The editor's debug build stops it, but the web's release build freezes (v0.81's opening froze at its stage change). Desktop harness runs can't catch this: check story or animation changes in the web export too.
- Non-resource files (`.tsx`, `.txt`, `.po`) only reach the export through `include_filter` in `export_presets.cfg`; `exclude_filter` keeps `test/*` and `addons/gut/*` out of every preset (PIX-351, `tools/test_export.py`).

## Collision and motion

- **Collision (PIX-137)**: the hero and enemies are `MOTION_MODE_FLOATING`; the hero's box is their feet (10x8, 3 px below the position). Anything that blocks must cover x 4..12, y 7..15 of each cell it blocks, so the hero's position (and `player_cell`) never enters a blocked cell (`test_puny_props` checks every tier). Villagers' bodies run 14 px below their centre. Every blocker the grid doesn't spell out goes in `MapData.covered` (props' extra cells, chests, placed furniture, solid field decor), which `is_walkable` reads, so spawns, pacing and arrivals agree with physics. In rooms, `PunyInterior.plan()["over"]` names furniture drawn over a cell (a bed's foot is the bed). Every villager's spot and pacing square is open ground on every map and at every age (`test_npcs`, PIX-342).
- **Motion (PIX-135)**: physics interpolation is on for the `actors` layer only (world root OFF). Anything that moves an actor outside physics must run on physics ticks (tweens: `set_process_mode(Tween.TWEEN_PROCESS_PHYSICS)`), and every time the hero is placed rather than walked, call `world.camera_rig.cut()` (`CameraRig.cut`: resets the hero's interpolation and cuts the camera). The camera (`scripts/world_camera.gd`) is top-level and eases toward where the hero is *drawn* (`_follow_hero`, from ticks the world records after the actors move) onto whole screen pixels; the zoom is fitted so an art pixel is whole screen pixels (`CameraRig.fit_zoom`). Do not turn on `snap_2d_transforms_to_pixel` and do not re-attach smoothing to the hero. `--screenshot --map town motion` measures it (`backsteps=`; the `motion` flow, windowed).
