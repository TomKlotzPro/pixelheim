# Art: Shade's packs, characters, ground, places, light, collision, motion

Read before touching how anything looks, a sprite, a tile, a shader, collision or the camera.

## Paid art (PIX-133, PIX-138)

- Shade's paid packs (Medieval Age; the Survival, Armor, Jewelry and Puny Skills icon packs) are NOT in this public repo: they live in the private `TomKlotzPro/pixelheim-assets` repo under `retro-rpg/shade/`, are installed by `scripts/fetch-private-art.sh` into git-ignored `godot/assets/puny/medieval/` and `godot/assets/puny/shade/`, and CI's `godot` job and the deploy fetch them with the deploy key secret `PIXELHEIM_ASSETS_KEY` (CI's windowed jobs never do: their pictures are public). **Never commit their PNGs, their sample maps, or anything cut from them.** Buying art is Tom's call.
- The game draws only Shade's art (plus our own pieces drawn in code in his palette: `*_art.gd`). Code must keep working without the paid packs (`PunyTown.available()` false: no houses, props or paid icons drawn, collision intact, chests from his CC0 dungeon sheet, rooms from his CC0 sheets by `PunyInterior.plan_free`/`furnish_free`, PIX-318); test pure logic through `PunyTown.plan()`, `PunyProps.plan()` and friends, which need no art. A harness run's `cc0` (and `PunyTown.withheld` in a test) holds the pack back where it's installed; the `free-rooms` flow enters every room that way. Never create a `PunyTown` layer without the pack (its sheet isn't there).
- Icons (PIX-138): `godot/assets/puny/icons.json` maps every item (by id), skill (by name, `<theme>/<n>` in the Puny Skills pack) and ailment to Shade's art: `free/<sheet>@c,r` (CC0 sheets in `assets/puny/icons/`, committed), `medieval@<tile>`, or a path under the private repo's `retro-rpg/shade/`. `ItemIcons` resolves them (null without the paid art). A new item or skill without an entry fails `test_item_icons`. New paid packs go in the private repo under `retro-rpg/<artist>/<pack>/` with a line in its `retro-rpg/README.md`.

## The town's buildings, rooms and props

- Houses: each roof cluster in the map becomes a 9-wide gable centred on the door (Shade's cottage grammar, height by repeating body rows) plus hip-roofed wings for the remaining columns; roof colours are fixed row shifts into the pack's colour blocks (`ROOF_ROWS`). Cells a house covers become `roof` in the grid, roof cells it leaves become `grass` (`freed`).
- Rooms (`scripts/puny_interior.gd`, `PunyInterior.plan`) get plank floors (stone in the smithy) and a rug, cream walls autotiled around the room, windows on the back wall, Shade's door, and furniture per tile (`FURNITURE`, `RUNS`); furniture spreading onto floor blocks it, except a bed's foot (the inn wakes guests there). What Shade's packs don't have a keeper's room draws in code (`RoomArt`: Liane's observatory, PIX-315; the ledgers lying open on their desks, PIX-338).
- Outdoor props (`scripts/puny_props.gd`, PIX-137, `PunyProps.plan`): lamps, wells, shrine, barrels, crates, counters, fences and flowers become Shade's torches, roofed well (a well in paving all round is the square's 2x2 fountain), the dungeon sheet's stone knight, market crates, autotiled log fences and flat flowers, y-sorted in `actors` on the bottom of their foot with a StaticBody2D exactly there; cells they stand on beyond the tile go in `MapData.covered`. Street lamps and lit windows are `LampArt` (PIX-311, drawn in code, so they stand without the pack). Door signs (`scripts/shop_sign.gd`, PIX-134) are hanging boards with a pack icon per label (`ShopSign.ICONS`).
- Paving is `Streets` (PIX-307, the Medieval Age's stone by the town's age), dirt roads wear by `RoadWear`.
- **Pixelheim's rampart is one wall (PIX-248, `Rampart`)**: the town map's top two rows and its outermost columns are the fields outside the wall (no walk reaches them; `test_ways` allows unreached ground at an edge), the wall is row 2 and columns 1 and 82, its gate the three doors at (39..41, 2) between two towers, and the Reach's way in sets the hero down at (40, 3). The Reach's gate is (47..49, 42), landing at (48, 41). `Rampart.plan(grid, ashen, gatehouses_only)` draws both (the Medieval Age's stone with its 16-piece mask, Shade's CC0 castle pieces without the pack), scorched while `Town.ruins` stand; the towers' tops block a cell above the wall (`covers`). Greyhold's south gate has the same gatehouse (`Rampart.GATEHOUSES`, PIX-337), the rest of its wall Shade's castle run.

## Settlements, dungeons, ways in

- **Each settlement its own (PIX-313, PIX-312)**: `Hamlet` (pure, keyed by map: `fishing` Saltmere, `fort` Greyhold, `frost` the Frostgate, `mine` Blackiron, `smithy` the mountain road) gives `PunyTown.plan(grid, style)` its houses' walls, a chimney on every roof (smoke rises from it: `MapView.chimney_of`) and the Frostgate's snowed roofs (`shaders/snowed_roof.gdshader`); every outdoor barrel and crate is drawn at the folk's scale (`Hamlet.store`, same feet); and `Hamlet.DRESSING` places its set dressing by hand: what stands blocks only open ground off the paths, never a villager's, a chest's, a pack's or a patch deck's cell nor a door's step, and never closes a way (`Hamlet.fits`, `test_hamlet`); what floats, hangs on a wall, sits on a roof or lies flat blocks nothing. Its pieces (boats, nets, the fish stall, gardens, ore, a cart, lanterns, Morvax's forge stack, the Kings' Vault's iron door, the post box and post house) are `HamletArt`; what the hubs grow (PIX-324) is `HubArt`, laid the same way. Report field `hamlet=`, flows `hamlet-*`.
- **Each dungeon looks like its place (PIX-310)**: `DungeonLook.plan(map, paid)` (pure, keyed by the floor's dungeon: `sea`, `mine`, `crypt`, `ice`, `vault`) dresses a cave floor without touching its map: flats on open floor (the mine's rail line, the crypt's bones and drains, the Vault's rugs), pieces on walls' faces (timber props, niches, cobwebs at true corners, banners, weed; never a torch), swaps drawn over cells that already block (ore carts, ore), the sea's pools (walkable, drawn by `shaders/dungeon.gdshader` from `DungeonLook.mask`), and with the Medieval Age each look's floor block. Our own pieces are `DungeonArt`. A planned floor's entrance takes one of `DungeonFloor.ENTRANCES` by its own dice (`entrance_of`); a new planned floor that opens in the same shape as another fails `test_dungeon_look` (retune `ENTRANCE_SALT`, which moves only entrances: test_depths' `PLANNED` pins change, `BEYOND` must not). A dungeon's way in is `EntranceArt` (PIX-290); a shut gate `GateArt` (PIX-254).
- An `ice` tile in a cave is walkable ice: MapView lays the floor under it again on a layer of its own with `shaders/frozen.gdshader` and a cold light (`Lights.ICE`) every few cells; keep ice off the walls but for columns standing in it.

## Characters

- Characters are Shade's Puny family at 1x (PIX-130): `PunyArt` maps roles, villagers and species to sheets. Row orders differ per family: Puny Characters and PunyMonsters turn clockwise (down 0, right 2, up 4, left 6), Mini World rows are down/up/right/left; sheets laid out otherwise are in `PunyArt.LAYOUTS` (PIX-243). Check a new sheet's facing on the art itself (a face, a tail, a swing's arc), not only a lineup capture. A new villager sprite id or monster species needs a `PunyArt` entry; `test_puny_art` fails otherwise.
- **Walks step with the ground (PIX-243)**: in the world, `Gait` sets the walk's frames from the distance covered (`PunyArt.WALKS`: cycle and stride per family), never the sprite's clock, and keeps the sprite paused while walking; a walker plays anything else through its `_play`, which halts the gait. The hero's walk is Shade's rise between his two steps (columns 1, 2, 1, 3). Off the world (title, rank-up), give a crossing walk `PunyArt.walk_speed_scale`.
- **Eight ways where Shade drew them (PIX-293)**: Puny Characters' rows 1, 3, 5, 7 are the ways between (`FAMILIES.puny.between`). The hero's walk is drawn eight ways (`Gait.drawn_way`/`steer_eight`, a 30-degree hold so a stick near a line doesn't flicker) and stands the way it left off; `facing` (swings, E, the hitbox) stays the four ways (`Gait.steer`), and a swing, a hurt or `player.face()` turns the drawing back to it. Any eight-way walker's quarter turn passes the way between for `TURN_SECONDS` (`Gait.between`). `--screenshot turning` (flow `turning`) reports `turns=` and `jumps=`.
- **The hero is drawn dressed (PIX-129)**: always get the hero's spec from `GameState.upkeep.hero_art()` (never `PunyArt.hero` for the player), which swaps in a worn helmet's head and armour's body from other Puny Characters sheets (`PunyArt.HEADS`/`BODIES`; the head is the figure's top 7 rows). A new head or body item needs an entry there; a look Shade didn't draw is a colour swap of one of his sheets in `godot/tools/outfits.py`, in its icon's own colours (PIX-335: the Wyrm Visor green like its icon). Check outfits with `--screenshot --map town --wear iron_helm,iron_armor`.
- **Things on the ground are half a cell (PIX-286)**: gathering patches and ground treasure are `PatchesLayer.pickup`: the 16 px icon at `PICKUP_SCALE` 0.5 on a small shadow, bobbing a pixel, with a glint. Chests stay full size. Look book `drops`.

## The ground

- The world is Shade's Puny World (`PunyTerrain`): corner wang tiles from his `.tsx` on the **dual grid** (layer shifted half a tile up-left, one tile per cell corner), so terrain edges sit on collision edges. Shade pairs terrains only with grass; `STRENGTH` settles other meetings. The tile layer only puts an invisible blocker on unwalkable cells (and the ruins' stone outdoors). A new ground tile id goes in `GROUND_TILES` and `PunyTerrain.GROUND`.
- **Open ground (PIX-305)**: the tint mask's alpha is a *kind*, not a coverage (`PunyTerrain.KIND_TONE`, `KIND_MIRE`, `KIND_FRINGE`): region_tint.gdshader gives each pixel one cell's tone whole, so never blend the mask or write a partial alpha (it left dither bands along every road through the ash). The ground layer's `ground_life` lays the patches, drifts, murk, puddles and the wind's ripples (`shaders/open_ground.gdshaderinc`); `OpenGround` mirrors its patch noise in GDScript to lay the Medieval Age's greens and snow as tufted patches (paid pack only; change both sides together, `test_open_ground` compares them). The small things in the grass, snow and mire are `Scatter.detail` picks drawn by `GroundDetail` on a tile layer, kept in `KeptGround` and off `MapView.bare`. Report fields `detail=` and `tone=`, flows `ground-*`.
- **Burnt land (PIX-308)**: `AshLand.burnt` gives each rock cell the burn of the open ground nearest it through the rock, kept in `KeptGround.burnt`: burnt rock is `TINTS["burnt_rock"]`, no crowns, and `AshArt`'s dead trees in place of the pines. The shader scorches ash-toned dirt and burnt rock's grassy top (`scorched_ground`). The Ash's `cinders` lie on MapView's unshaded `ember_layer` (`shaders/embers.gdshader`), so they glow at night. Blackiron's workings are data in `AshLand.WORKINGS`. Ground healed round a lamp (`world.md`) takes the earth's tone and no scorch. Report field `scorch=`.
- Field decor (`scripts/scatter.gd`) and the woods (`Woods`, PIX-309, with `shaders/canopy_shade`) stand on the ground; solid decor blocks only where every neighbour stays open (`test_scatter`).
- `TileSetAtlasSource` animation separation counts **tiles**, not pixels; Puny water frames sit 2-4 rows apart and may overlap other tiles' strips, so `PunyTerrain._slot` opens a new source on the same sheet when one is full. Shade's animations that pause or loop back can't be TileSet atlas animations; `PunySheet` holds them on their first frame.

## Light, shaders and fire

- **The desktop app's canvas is linear light (PIX-227, `DesktopLook`)**: on Forward+ in a window (the app, harness runs, the look book's `--desktop`) the root viewport draws in HDR, so a shader's screen reads, `COLOR` and constants are linear there. Every shader that does colour maths over the world includes `shaders/linear.gdshaderinc` and reads with `to_display` / writes with `to_canvas` (`test_desktop_look` fails otherwise); it compiles away in the browser. Light colours go through `Lights.make` (kept linear by the LightRig). Read pixels or save pictures through `DesktopLook.snapshot` / `DesktopLook.shown`, never the raw viewport image. Don't reach for a WorldEnvironment glow: on 4.7 everything drawn after its pass, the HUD included, came out lifted. The app's wider glow marks the brights in the screen's alpha (`glow_mask`) and must be the first screen reader on its own canvas layer (`glow_wide`).
- **A shade over the world takes no dark of its own**: the woods' shade multiplied the ground by the night a second time, and a lighter band showed along every map line at night (PIX-330); it is `unshaded` now. The night's dark and the lights are the LightRig's alone; keep lights per block within `test_night_light`'s budget (close lamps share one light, `Lamps.lit_among`).
- In a `canvas_item` shader, `COLOR` in `fragment()` already holds texel × modulate; sampling `TEXTURE` again squares it (everything goes dark).
- **Fire is ours (PIX-327)**: `FireArt` draws flames in Shade's fire colours (`tools/fire.py`) on what burns, with smoke, sparks and coals in whole pixels (`shaders/fire_*`); Reduce motion calms them. Flows `ash-fires`, `ash-fires-still`.
- **Bind every tween to the node it animates** (`node.create_tween()`), above all a looping one: a `set_loops()` tween owned by a parent that outlives its target spins forever once the target is freed. The editor's debug build stops it, but the web's release build freezes (v0.81's opening froze at its stage change). Desktop harness runs can't catch this: check story or animation changes in the web export too.
- Non-resource files (`.tsx`, `.txt`, `.po`) only reach the export through `include_filter` in `export_presets.cfg`; `exclude_filter` keeps `test/*` and `addons/gut/*` out of every preset (PIX-351, `tools/test_export.py`).

## Collision and motion

- **Collision (PIX-137)**: the hero and enemies are `MOTION_MODE_FLOATING`; the hero's box is their feet (10x8, 3 px below the position). Anything that blocks must cover x 4..12, y 7..15 of each cell it blocks, so the hero's position (and `player_cell`) never enters a blocked cell (`test_puny_props` checks every tier). Villagers' bodies run 14 px below their centre. Every blocker the grid doesn't spell out goes in `MapData.covered` (props' extra cells, chests, placed furniture, solid field decor), which `is_walkable` reads, so spawns, pacing and arrivals agree with physics. In rooms, `PunyInterior.plan()["over"]` names furniture drawn over a cell (a bed's foot is the bed). Every villager's spot and pacing square is open ground on every map and at every age (`test_npcs`, PIX-342).
- **Motion (PIX-135)**: physics interpolation is on for the `actors` layer only (world root OFF). Anything that moves an actor outside physics must run on physics ticks (tweens: `set_process_mode(Tween.TWEEN_PROCESS_PHYSICS)`), and every time the hero is placed rather than walked, call `world.camera_rig.cut()` (`CameraRig.cut`: resets the hero's interpolation and cuts the camera). The camera (`scripts/world_camera.gd`) is top-level and eases toward where the hero is *drawn* (`_follow_hero`, from ticks the world records after the actors move) onto whole screen pixels; the zoom is fitted so an art pixel is whole screen pixels (`CameraRig.fit_zoom`). Do not turn on `snap_2d_transforms_to_pixel` and do not re-attach smoothing to the hero. `--screenshot --map town motion` measures it (`backsteps=`; the `motion` flow, windowed).
