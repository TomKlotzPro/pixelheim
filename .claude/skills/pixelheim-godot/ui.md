# UI: screens, the look, the HUD, words on the screen, input

Read before touching a screen, the HUD, anything with text or keys.

## Screens

- **Every menu, page and conversation extends `Screen` (`scripts/screen.gd`, PIX-136)**: build it in `_open()` (not `_ready`), set `closing_actions` for its own key (`[&"inventory"]`), return what a key does from `_command(event)` (an empty `Callable` lets it pass on to closing), and leave with `close()`. Screens *hold* the world rather than pausing it: holds are counted and the world runs again only when the last screen lets go, so never write `get_tree().paused` in a screen (only right before `reload_current_scene`). Open the hero's screens through `world.open_screen(name)`.
- **A screen that dims the world has the whole canvas (PIX-346)**: `dim()` puts the HUD's layer aside (the dock, the clock, the quill, every line over the world) while it's open, counted like the holds and brought back as it was when the last of them goes. The title, the rank-up and every cutscene (PIX-368: a sign's plate stood half under the letterbox) put it aside too; the dawn keeps it (its sky is the HUD's). `Layout.over` finds an open screen's words on the HUD's dock, clock or quill (`Hud.shown`), and the harness's `overflow` counts them, so every `fit-*` flow holds it.
- Keys reach the top screen only (the highest layer, the latest among equals, PIX-199). `Screen` marks the key handled **before** it runs the command `_command` returned: an action that reloads the scene frees the menu (calling it after logs `!is_inside_tree`), and a key must never reach a second screen (PIX-131).
- **Esc goes back one level**: a screen opened from a menu is its child a layer over it (`Screen.open_over`, the pause menu's and the title's alike; the pause menu's card and keys hide meanwhile, so none of it shows round a smaller screen such as Saves), never opened after closing the menu (PIX-427: Saves did, so Esc went on to play); flows `title-*-back`, `pause-*-back` hold it. A language chosen in Options reloads the world in it, and the title or the pause menu opens Options again on that row of that tab (`back_at`, `back_tab`, PIX-428; no flow can: a reload starts the harness over, so `test_pause_menu` and play.py check it).
- Screens ease in on their own: the world hooks every `*_screen.gd` CanvasLayer it adds into `UiStyle.enter` (fade + 10 px rise, skipped with reduce motion; the title and rank-up make their own entrances), which also plays `Sound.play("ui_open")` and `"ui_close"`; move keys tick while a screen pauses the tree, unless the move made a sound of its own (a page turned, a refusal: PIX-383). Those sounds are blips synthesized in `sound.gd` (`UI_SOUNDS`).

## The look (`UiStyle`, `scripts/ui_style.gd`, PIX-138)

- Pages of a village ledger. Parchment (`WINDOW`) in a carved wooden frame with brass corner studs, rows and slots (`CARD`) inked on the page with a `RIM` line, wooden planks for buttons (`UiStyle.plank`, `UiStyle.focus(button, on)` for the chosen one). Frames are pixel art generated from the palette at 1x and shown at `UI_SCALE` 2, as 9-slice `StyleBoxTexture`s (the page tiles its grain, never stretches it).
- **GegX's kit dresses the menus when it's installed (PIX-385)**: `UiStyle._kit(piece)` builds a 9-slice from `assets/puny/gegx/` at scale 1 (the kit is drawn for canvas pixels; never through `_texture`, which doubles) with `ui_kit.json`'s margins and content margins, and returns null when it isn't installed or `UiStyle.withheld` is set (the harness's `cc0`); every builder then draws today's frame. `window()` wears its window (padding 0 or above 12) or panel (up to 12; the dock's `WOOD` plate stays drawn), `plank()`/`button()` its button states (a real pressed one), `focus()` its red chosen button, `UiStyle.tab(chosen)` and `UiStyle.tab_button(button, chosen)` its tabs, `UiStyle.slot(colourway, drawn)` its slots (`ready`, `spent`, `focus`, `fine`, `epic`: the dock's skills and portrait, what's worn), `UiStyle.bar_frame()` the boss bar's and the dock's bars' frame. Its buttons and tabs have page faces, so words on them take `UiStyle.plank_ink(lit)` (ink and rubric red with the kit, cream and gold without), never `CREAM`/`GOLD` by hand. Its frames are thicker (a window's words sit 22 in, a slot 60 round a 32 px icon): check a screen with the kit and with `cc0` (`test_ui_kit`; the `fit-*` flows both ways). Rows, cards, plates, keycaps, the tooltip, scrollbars and the cursor stay drawn.
- **Every screen's pages alike (PIX-388)**: pages stand between the title's margins (x 80 to 1200, the map's frame and waypoints too: `MapScreen.MAP_BOX` and `LIST_BOX` in `window(UiStyle.INSET)`), words and lists `UiStyle.INSET` (24) inside a page's edge in either look (`window(UiStyle.INSET)`, or placed by hand off a page's rect: `InventoryScreen.PAGE`, `LedgerScreen.PAGE`, `ShopScreen`'s rects, `CodexScreen.CARD`, `SkillsScreen.PAGE`, `JournalScreen.AT`), side by side `UiStyle.GAP` (16) apart, with a title (and a line under it at y 66) over them; tabs stand on a page's top edge, `INSET` in from its corner, touching, never overlapping (the kit's tab takes 13 a side, a pixel clear of its frame; its art has a clear shoulder, 4 at the left, so the pack's row starts 4 early and its eight French tabs end `INSET` clear of the right corner; `test_fit`'s `test_tabs_stand_apart_on_their_pages_edge` checks the rows). A row of tabs wider than its page goes on along the next page's edge, never across a corner (PIX-543: `ShopScreen.tab_spots`, Hilda's fifth at Buy), and tabs on a page that stands as high as the title start a gap past it (the journal's). Every plank is `UiStyle.PLANK` (44) tall, a keyed one's cap inside its face, never over its frame: the kit's face is 22 px between its trims, so with the kit the cap is slim (PIX-395: `keycap(key, small, slim)`, drawn on the kit's 1x grid, 20 tall, a pixel clear; `test_ui_kit` checks it, pressed too). A count on a tab and a skill's key on its slot are `UiStyle.tag` (a dark tag 18 tall astride the edge). A tab that can't be chosen is the tab dimmed (`tab_button`). Every full screen's footer ends on its own key and Esc, `close` (the pause menu's too); the rank-up's fork rides in its letterbox on `UiStyle.footer`'s caps.
- **Options are four tabs (PIX-546)**: Sound, Display, Access, Controls (`OptionsScreen.TABS`), the journal's chips on the page's top edge. Left and right adjust a row there, so the tabs are a place the keys stand: Options open on them (`on_tabs`), left and right turn them, down or E goes into the rows, and past a tab's first or last row is the tabs again; no shoulder buttons (they are the fight's). A new option is a row in `_build()` with its `tab`; a tab holds seven rows a column at most. The report's `tab=` says the tab and the row (`display row Rim light`), and the `options-*` and `fit-options-*` flows hold each tab. **Every row and tab says what it does (PIX-550)**: a row's dict carries `help`, one or two plain sentences written from what the setting's readers do (what it changes, what Off gives), and `TAB_HELP` a line a tab; the line stands under the rows in the reading type (`help`, the report's `help=`), so a new row needs its line, its French, and room for it in French with Large reading text (`test_options_say_what_each_tab_and_row_does`).
- **Two text palettes, by surface**: on the page `INK`, `FADED`, and `LAMP` (rubric red) for focus; on the dark around windows (headings, subtitles, gold counts, footers, the title, anything over the world) `CREAM`, `DUSK`, `GOLD`. Rarity inks are `FINE` and `EPIC`. Content that isn't in a window sits on `UiStyle.page(rect)`, never on the backdrop. `UiStyle.window()` is a main window; `UiStyle.box(fill, rim, padding)` infers the rest (a `LAMP` rim means focus, `CARD` with padding ≥ 12 means a window, anything else is a card). Footers are `UiStyle.footer("Esc  close      W/S  choose", at)` (keycaps, on the dark). **A footer names only the keys that act where the choice stands (PIX-484)**: a screen whose keys change with the row, the tab or a question asked builds its line in `_set_footer` and shows it through `UiStyle.refooted(self, footer, line)` (the same footer while it says the same, for the same hands; `Screen._hands_changed` asks again when a pad is taken up or put down), put together with `+`, never a format (`test_i18n`), each piece `keys  words` so `i18n.py` finds its words (`FOOTER_PIECE`): the counter while it asks how many, the pack's Craft tab (E on a recipe only where its row says E crafts it: at its station, with its makings), the Skills page (E where it learns or walks, 1-6 where a key takes the card), a save's slot by what it holds, the stats' "spend a point (3 left)" and no E with none, Options' rows (a slider's left and right, a switch's E too, a key's row: left and right cross to the other column, only E rebinds; `test_menus_keys`). Don't hand-roll `StyleBoxFlat`s or colours.
- **Type is Nunito for every word read, pixel type for two things (PIX-533, PIX-549)**: `UiStyle.label(text, size, color)` gives Nunito at `TEXT` (20) for any size below 24 and `BIG` (30) from 24, whole pixels only; `UiStyle.strong` is the bold cut; `UiStyle.sized(control, size)` applies the same rule to buttons and fields and keeps each size with its own face (`body_font(big)`/`bold_font(big)`: a `FontVariation` whose line box is cut to the pixel face's, 22 at TEXT and 33 at BIG, `UiStyle.TRIM`/`TRIM_BIG`, since Nunito's own 29 and 42 ran Options off the screen; the spacing is in pixels, so a face drawn at the other size has the wrong box); `UiStyle.heading` is the bold cut at BIG for screen titles (18 and up), TEXT for sections. **The story's title cards are Nunito Bold too (PIX-534)**: `UiStyle.titled(label, small)` sets `UiStyle.title_font()` (the bare bold face, its own line box, since a card stands alone) at `TITLE` (60) or `TITLE_SMALL` (44) and nothing else: a chapter's title (the smaller when its line, in the player's language, runs past `ChapterScreen.TITLE_ROOM`; `test_satchel` holds every title in EN and FR), the dawn's "Pixelheim", a cutscene's name (small, in capitals); the title's menu planks are the bold cut at TEXT (`sized(plank, TEXT, true)`). Nunito draws grey antialiased with light hinting and no subpixel positioning, no MSDF, no `fixed_size`; it has no arrows, so Pixeloid stands behind it as its fallback. Pixel type stays in two places: the PIXELHEIM logo, on the title and slammed down in the opening (`UiStyle.logo_font()`, Press Start 2P, used by nothing else), and `UiStyle.pixel_font()` (Pixeloid Sans Bold at its own 9 px, scaled whole) for every keycap's letter (18, 2x, as its cap; the world keycap and the touch "!" at 9, enlarged 3x by the camera). The numbers and words floating over the world are Nunito Bold since PIX-549 (below). Never set a raw, fractional or odd size on a Nunito label (only TEXT and BIG through `sized`, TITLE and TITLE_SMALL through `titled`, `WorldFx.FLOAT_PX` for the floats), never add a second body face, a third weight or an italic, and size pages and rows for 20 px text (about 9 px per character, narrower than Pixeloid's 18): when a line doesn't fit, move the detail out of the row rather than shrinking it or the trim. VT323 and blackletter were tried and dropped.
- **Tooltips, scrollbars and the mouse are the look's too (PIX-384)**: `UiStyle.setup()` puts the tooltip's slip (`UiStyle.tooltip()`, ink on parchment in brass) and the slim brass-on-wood scrollbar in Godot's default theme (the root's theme stops at a screen's CanvasLayer), and hands `Input` the brass arrow and hand (`UiStyle.cursor`, Images at 2x, hotspots `ARROW_TIP`/`HAND_TIP`). `UiStyle.button` takes the hand. A tooltip is a window that samples as its owner does: give the control with `tooltip_text` `texture_filter = TEXTURE_FILTER_NEAREST`, or its words blur.
- **A list keeps to its page (PIX-298)**: in a row the name gives way (`Layout.ellipsis`, or `Layout.shortened(label, room)` when the whole name must show elsewhere), tags, prices and keys keep their words and wrap past a share of the row (`Layout.narrowed`); a long list scrolls in a window on its page. `Layout.overflows` (every `fit-*` flow, `test_fit`) also fails a scrolling window or a free list pushed wider than it was laid out (`Layout.spills`), however far inside the canvas, and a screen's piece standing on one of its pieces marked `Layout.CLEAR` (`Layout.crowded`, PIX-373: the ledger's status line and keys, which its info card grew under; opt-in per screen). The ledger's info says the chosen row's word only, never every row's, and says it first (PIX-488: the hall's, last, was the part a long card hid; `fit-hall` and `fit-hall-en` expect `reading=` empty, so a card that runs long at the normal size fails). **A card keeps its size (PIX-477, PIX-483)**: words that run past it (Large reading text, `UiStyle.reading`, or French) scroll in a `ScrollContainer` inside the card, `Layout.paging(scroll, event)` first in the screen's `_command` (Page Up and Page Down, by their key codes: the window's whole lines less one of the words' own height, `Layout.line_of`, so a page starts on a line; What's new pages through it too, `Layout.turned` for its left and right; W/S and A/D are the rows' and the tabs'), and the footer names the keys only while `Layout.runs_long(scroll)` (`Layout.paged_footer`, rebuilt on the scrollbar's `changed`): the ledgers' `info_scroll`, the counter's `about`; the harness's `large` turns the setting on and its report's `reading=` says how far the card is paged, `keyed`, and `pad` when the caps are the pad's. **A paged card ends on a whole line, clear of its scrollbar (PIX-543)**: its scroll goes on the card through `Layout.lined(scroll)` (a holder that draws the window back to the top of the line its foot would cut, looked at every frame: a label still settles a frame after the scrollbar says its words changed) and its words through `Layout.beside_bar(words)` (`BAR_GAP`); what E costs stays out of what scrolls (the counter's Price and Gold after stand over its keys). **A pad pages by its right stick, on every screen (PIX-487)**: pushed up or down, a page a push (`Controls.paged`, which `Layout.paging` asks: the shoulders are the fight's, the left stick and the D-pad the rows' and the tabs'; pushed past `PAD_PUSH`, let go under the lower `PAD_LET_GO`, so a push trembling about the mark is one page, and `note_device` hears it let go with no screen open); the footer's caps are `{key:page_up}/{key:page_down}`, PgUp/PgDn or the pad's R↑/R↓ (`--keys pad_pgdn`, flows `pad-hall-large*`). A footer already full gives a key's place to them: `Layout.footer_for(scroll, line, paging_line)` shows the shorter line while the card pages (the pack's leaves R's sort out: French ran past the canvas, `fit-pack-long-large`), and a footer `UiStyle` had to close up takes its own width again (`reset_size`), or a centred one stands off the middle and past the edge. **Every reading text grows with Large reading text (PIX-484)**, not some of a panel: a description that keeps its place on a page (the pack's under its list, the journal's chosen quest, the Skills page's) is a `Layout.reader(label, width)` given its words by `Layout.read(reader, text, most, least)`, as tall as its lines up to `most` and paged past that like a card (the screen's `reader`, which `reading=` finds too); the pack's list gives it rows (`LIST_LARGE`), the Skills tree closes its rows up, and every line of the counter's card is `UiStyle.reading`, a piece's numbers under the hero then (flows `fit-*-large*`). A conversation's window grows upward instead, its foot where it stands (three lines of Large French run past its 192 px): it hangs by its foot in a frame that keeps its place, because `UiStyle.enter` settles a screen's own children where they stood at opening, so a screen's child that moves itself as it lays out is put back (flows `fit-talk-large*`). A name the player typed gives way where it's shown (`Layout.ellipsis`: under hero creation's portrait, PIX-481; the dock). `Layout.overflows` also fails a one-line label cut at its room's end with no ellipsis (`Layout.cut`, PIX-482): give it an ellipsis when the whole shows elsewhere, wrap it (`Layout.wrapped`) where there's room, or say less (the Skills page's line for the path walked is its name; the blurb reads under the tree). French runs longer: check a screen with `--lang fr` (or `--pseudo`) too.

## The HUD

- **The HUD is `Hud` (`scripts/world_hud.gd`, `world.hud`)**; its dock (`scripts/hud_dock.gd`, `world.hud.dock`) is a wooden plate centred on the bottom edge (PIX-388): an XP strip along its top (notched in tenths, its numbers under the mouse), the portrait in a slot, name and rank, HP and energy in framed bars (`UiStyle.bar_frame()`, or a brass rim drawn without the kit) with their numbers beside, six skill slots by number key (each key on a dark `UiStyle.tag` over the slot's top edge, clear of the icon kit or not; its cost on another at the foot corner, digits alone, red when the hero can't pay, PIX-442; while a pad plays, skills 1-3 read its LB, LT and RT and 4-6, which it takes from the menu, none; after a cast a veil over the icons draws back up as the skills' shared turn runs out), gold, and a Menu (the pad's Back cap beside it while a pad plays, its room kept either way so the dock's width never follows the hands) that opens a panel of every screen over the "Next" line (`HudDock.words_top`, PIX-388: it hid the line's end), an icon (`ItemIcons.screen`, icons.json's `screens`), a name and a key each (on a pad its own button, the pack's Y, or no cap), lit on a rubric card for the pad's choice and on a plain card under the mouse, and lights when points wait. In Large reading text the "Next" line and the message plate stand `HudDock.NEXT_LARGE` higher, their foot as far off the dock's rim as at the normal size (PIX-543). Nothing else sits at the bottom. `world.hud.dock.refresh()` after anything the hero shows changes. `--screenshot dockmenu` opens its menu; `--keys back,pad_down` opens it as a pad does and steers it, and the report's `dockmenu=` lists its lines, the chosen one in brackets. The place's nameplate near a shop door is `Hud.update_nameplate`; the clock and the quill sit top right (`saves.md`).
- **The Next line and the message plate share one place over the dock, in turn (PIX-543, PIX-537)**: a message waits `Messages.GIVE_WAY_S` while the line fades out, is laid out meanwhile (`Hud.update_objective` fits it from the moment it is `up()`, so it never shows unfitted), and the line comes back once it has gone (`Hud.line_shows`). On the Night of Ash the line names the key its step teaches, so it stays and the message stands over it; a plain message from before the night's step fades out when the step moves on (`Messages._old_news`). Don't fade the two across each other.
- **The marks over the world are pixel art in that palette (`Marks`, PIX-284, PIX-289)**: the talk mark over a giver (`Npc.mark_sprite`: a bubble on a pin's tail, "!"/"?", parchment, gold for the main story by `Quests.mark_tells_story`, faded while under way), the danger burst over a foe that notices (`EnemyDrawing.alert_mark`, cold blue for a fright), and the HUD's arrow toward the bearing (`Marks.arrow`, drawn for eight ways and never rotated, at UI_SCALE; never within `Hud.ARROW_CLEAR` of the hero, PIX-462). They're rows of letters drawn at 1x, ringed in `NIGHT`, lit from the top left; centre them on heads by their middle columns (`Npc.mark_spot`, `Marks.centred`, `Ink`). Don't go back to font glyphs or turned polygons over the world. **Each mark over its owner, none on the hero (PIX-515)**: a villager's body begins as high as their head (`Npc.body_of`), so the hero stops over it from above as from the side, 15 px off at most (`BODY_TOP`: the middle of the row above stays walked, flows `stand-above` and `walk-above`, so the tallest's head reaches a few pixels up the hero's feet); E's plate stands over a villager's head in their mark's place, which waits while the plate shows (`Npc.prompted`), and at their side with the hero above (`Interaction.folk_spot`); a foe's "!" stands right over its head, its level tag waiting until it has gone (`EnemyDrawing.mark_foot`, a named foe's over its words still), and makes way for a hero under it as the tag does.

## Words on the screen (`TextBudget`, PIX-334, PIX-343)

- **One rule** (`scripts/state/text_budget.gd`, pure, tested): an area of the screen shows at most `room` things at a time and the rest wait their turn (nothing is pushed off unread); the same thing again folds into the one showing or waiting ("+3 gold" four times reads "+12 gold", a quest's count takes the newest); in a fight what can wait (`later`: a quest's count, a level, the Post) waits until it's over, and while a title card is up (PIX-378: at a boss's fall the level's plate makes way for the Victory card, `Messages.make_way`, LEVEL UP rising saying it meanwhile). `Messages` (`world_messages.gd`) keeps the battle log (bottom-left, one short line at a time since PIX-343, each held `UiStyle.reading_seconds`) and the message plate through it.
- Words over the world step up just clear of a level tag or a word already there (`TextBudget.rise`, PIX-340: a pack shows one level tag, its highest; with no room within its most, back down to the lowest clear height, PIX-378), under the HUD's plates at the top (`room_under`). A kill's gold, XP and loot rise where it fell (`Gains`, `world_fx.gd`; PIX-245, PIX-343), LEVEL UP over the hero. A title card stands clear of what rises (`TextBudget.spot`, `Hud.title_card`, PIX-345). Nothing rises on the hero's figure or a foe's "!" (`Foes` hands both to `WorldFx.keep_clear`, PIX-515): the hero's own number and a win step up over their head, a blow's number on a foe never does (it read as damage taken) and comes up at the foe's side instead, there too when a win or a word has the place over the foe (`WorldFx.step_aside`; the far side all the same when both are taken); blows joined into one number count up where the first stood; a big word keeps `BIG_MARGIN` of air; a level's burst is drawn behind the hero (`skill_flash`'s `under`).
- **A kill's win pops (PIX-492, `LootPop`, `scripts/loot_pop.gd`)**: `WorldFx.burst_gains(gains, feet, grand)` is a kill's `show_gains`. Spoils has paid all of it and `floated` tallies it whole at the kill (the purse, saves, quests, `gold=` and `floats=` never wait); only the look takes its time: coins spring out of the foe (`LootBurst.coins`: by its gold, 8 at most, 16 for a `grand` win, a boss's or a named monster's), bounce once, glint, then sweep to the hero and tick (`coin_gain`, `tuned` a little higher each one, off the game's dice; the purse's own ring at the kill is held for them, `Soundscape.quiet_gold`, and only a tick `Sound.SAME_GAP_MS` after the last is played and counted in the run, so a boss's sixteen still climb a step a tick), sparks rise for its XP, and each one that lands hands its share to the float (`show_gains(share, at, false)`), which joins as a pack's wins always did: one "+N gold · +N XP" counting up in place, ending on what the kill paid (`LootBurst.shares` add up; `test_loot_pop`). The float comes up as the first spark lands (about half a second after the fall), its items' rows with it, stepped clear of the words born since the blow; `WorldFx.reach` counts the floats still to come (`LootPop.to_come`), so a title card stands clear of them. A drop hops out, lands and lies a moment before it goes to the pack: nothing for a common one, a soft glow for a fine one, a short beam for an epic one (`LootBurst.shine`, in `GAIN_TONES`), and it rings by rarity at the kill (a common piece is silent). Everything is drawn by one node from pooled entries, never a node a coin (past `LootPop.MOST` in the air the shares land at once), on dice of its own. A coin and a spark are a few world pixels on the world's grid (never `UiStyle.coin` at a float's scale: its pixels aren't the world's); a drop on the ground is a pouch on the same grid, rimmed in its rarity's colour (`_pouch`; its icon and name are the float's row above: an item's icon at a float's scale isn't the world's pixels either). Its glow and beam draw behind it (`show_behind_parent`). Each entry keeps the map it sprang on (`world.view`): what is in the air through a door stays behind (over one of the Reach's seamless edges it lands at once, `settle`, so the float still comes up), and the next map's first kill bursts as any other. Unshaded, so the night doesn't swallow them. **With Reduce motion nothing flies**: `burst_gains` is `show_gains`. Only a kill bursts: a chest, a patch, a catch float as they did. The report's `loot=` (`coins 6 flying 3 sparks 4 drops 1:epic counted 5/8 big`) and `--loot <rarity>` (a staged win: no run is sure of a fine or an epic drop) are `features/loot_pop/`'s, the flows `loot-*`.
- **A line shows at its moment or not at all (PIX-476)**: what is only true now goes through `Messages.log_now(line, holds)` (an ailment landed, an elite's howl: `holds` says its foe still stands; the hero's ailments purged: none since): it shows as it lands, a hint stepping aside for it (the roll's alone is gone for good once it stood `HINT_READ_S`; any other comes back after the line for its full `HINT_SECONDS`), and is dropped once `holds` fails, its foe is freed (PIX-503: kept by id as it lands, a freed foe's lambda is never asked, since it reads freed memory) or it has waited `NOW_S` behind the plate, or `NOW_MARGIN_S` past the reading time of the log's line it landed behind (a boss's roar), never shown late. "Quest complete" takes its own quest's "Quest accepted" off the plate at once (`_quest_done`). What a player needs to read (a level, a quest done, a boss's words) still waits its turn through `log_lines` and `flash`.
- **A line's rules are written into the catalogues (PIX-478, PIX-484)**: `tools/i18n.py`'s `set_line` sets every msgstr of `fr.po` and, for English, `locale/en.po` (generated: only the strings a rule changes), and `--check` fails on a line not set; nothing sets a line as the game runs. The rules: the spaced hyphen, the house's dash, after a no-break space (a line ends on it, never starts with it), and the hyphen before a French question's or cry's last word a no-break one (« êtes-vous ? » whole). So `Text.t` and the data's words come back with the dash held, in English too: a test compares `"built\u00a0- and"`, and a line built in code types its own (`Bearing.line`). **Never register a GDScript object in an engine server** (a `Translation` subclass in `TranslationServer`): the engine frees it after the scripts are gone and every run crashes at quit. `Layout.stranded(label)` finds a wrapped line that starts with ? ! : ; » or a dash, and `Layout.overflows` counts it, so every `fit-*` flow holds it (said as `^ ?!:;-`); the editor's build never breaks before ? ! ; : (ICU's rules), so only an export without its data could show those, and play.py's picture is the proof there. `Text.percent(n)` writes a share (« 70 % ») and `Text.s(n)` a plural's s (« 0 point »).

## Sound

Sounds are WAVs in `godot/assets/audio/` listed in `assets/data/audio.json`: the first ones rendered once from the old web synth, the newer by `tools/synth.py` (PIX-158, seeded). Music crossfades per place (`world_soundscape.gd`), the old and the new together on an equal-power curve (in dB they dipped some 17 dB halfway), a place's track taken up where it was left (`"resume"`), an intro before the loop with `"loopBegin"` (PIX-411).
- **The music** (PIX-416): `Sound.track_for` gives a place's or a fight's slot (audio.json's "tracks": `PLACE_TRACKS`, `DUNGEON_TRACKS`, the dragon for a boss on the square; PIX-498: `ashnight` everywhere while the Night of Ash runs, its fights under it, until the dawn's theme and the town's tune, the game's own, a D minor march `tools/synth.py` renders from the `"voices"` on its track, written as a theme's are, squares at duty 0.5 since a loop keeps its offset; harness `music` puts `music=` in the report); Leohpaz's RPG music bundle plays each where it's installed (`assets/audio/paid/music_<slot>.ogg` and `music.json`, `cc0` holds them back), the game's chiptune WAV without it, a slot with no WAV of its own its `"fallback"`'s (`Sound.heard_as`; the music playing for a slot sharing it carries on). `assets/audio/paid_music.json` names each slot's and theme's track ([zip, path], an `"intro"` joined at its handover, the runner-up and why); `tools/music_prep.py` (`python3 -I`, zips in `~/playground/leohpaz-music/zips/`) levels each to the manifest's `"loudness"` (-34.5 LUFS integrated: v0.307's tracks as heard) under its `"peak"`, and writes Ogg Vorbis, not MP3: Godot starts an MP3 part-way in by decoding up to there on the main thread (15 to 70 ms for a resumed track), a Vorbis in 1 to 2. ffmpeg's own Vorbis encoder pads a track's end up to 64 samples, so the prep pads it first with its loop's own next samples and moves loopBegin past them; Godot lands a loop on the sample its offset falls in, so `_music_of` adds half a sample. Both music players stream on the web too (`playback_type`, PIX-416 review): as samples, the web's default, a track was decoded whole on the main thread at its first play, kept as PCM for good and looped from its start, not its loopBegin (`perf.md`). On the web a change of place hushes the music before the map is built (PIX-417, `Sound.hush`, on `hushes_builds`): mixed there on the main thread 50 ms ahead, it was held mid-phrase at full level for as long as a map took to build (a gate's first visit about 300 ms, a room's door 75-100), then went on where it stopped. A dissolve or a cut waits `HUSH_S` (0.15 s) and a frame first (`world._hushed`, `Sound.hush_first`), the hero held still on the door meanwhile as on the desktop (PIX-425: walking on, they were over the town's wall when the place changed), a dark fade hushes as it darkens, a fall hushes before the inn is built (`on_player_died`), and the music comes back over half a second once the build's frame has passed (counted as one frame, as `Dissolve` counts it). The desktop mixes on its own thread and builds at once. A new way to change place goes through `_through_door`/`_through_dark`; a map built where no door is (a scene's place: the dawn, the ending, the morning after the Night of Bells; Lantern Night's stalls; a place grown round the hero after the board or the post) first awaits `world.hushed_build()` (PIX-420), the same wait, the screen held in the dark its `_fade_in` starts from, so the place left isn't seen again; dropped, as a door is, while a change of place already holds the hush, except a story's (`hushed_build(true)`: the dawn, the ending, the morning after the Night of Bells), which waits it out, the story having moved on. A screen that starts the world over (another slot, a language, the title, a new hero) calls `Screen.reload_world()`: Sound outlives the reload, so it hushes first too (`Sound.hush_first`), and the screen takes nothing more meanwhile. The bundle's story themes outlast their scenes (the ending's 157 s against a 46 s scene), so `Cutscene.finish` calls `Sound.end_theme()`: a theme still playing hands over to its `then` (victory) as its end would have; one with no `then` (the dawn) plays on until the world's music replaces it. Keep the music within the manifest's `"budgetMiB"` (the web fetches all of it, after the start); `test_audio.py` and `test_sound.gd` check the levels, the budget and the loops with the bundle, and skip a slot the private repo has that this build doesn't.
- **The web's audio packs** (PIX-518): the web build starts without the paid audio, which was 47 of its 62 MB pck. The Web preset's `exclude_filter` leaves `assets/audio/paid/*.wav` and `*.ogg` out of `index.pck` (their lists, `takes.json` and `music.json`, stay in: that is how the game knows a build has packs); `tools/audio_packs.gd` (run by the deploy and CI after the export) writes them as Godot imported them (each sound's `.import` and what it names under `.godot/imported/`) into `export/web/sfx.pck` (the takes, 5.4 MiB) and `music.pck` (the tracks, 40 MiB), git-ignored like the rest of `export/`, in the built site only. On the web `Sound._ready` fetches `Sound.PACKS` in that order with the browser's own `fetch` (`_fetch_packs`; not HTTPRequest: GitHub Pages gzips a `.pck`, the browser unzips it, and HTTPRequest counts the unzipped bytes against the zipped Content-Length; and Godot's client reads one stream chunk a frame), writes each to `/tmp` (the browser's memory; `user://` is IndexedDB) and mounts it (`Sound.mount`: `ProjectSettings.load_resource_pack`, then `arrived`), 13-17 ms each, one frame, and says so in the browser's console (`Sound: music.pck mounted (228 events and 23 tracks from the packs)`). Until a pack lands, and when one doesn't come, every sound is the game's own, as without the packs. Landing changes nothing that plays: the next take of an event is the packs', the next change of track the bundle's from its start (`_resume_at` cleared, `_heard` forgotten so a slot sharing the tune playing changes to its own), a bed's at the next `set_bed`. The desktop presets ship everything in the one pck. Never add a bus for this (PIX-412). play.py's `sound` (`Sound.report`) says what plays and how many events and slots the packs fill. A new kind of paid audio: put it in a pack in `audio_packs.gd`'s `PACKS` and the Web preset's exclude filter (`test_export.py` holds the two together).
- **Events** (PIX-411): the game plays moments, never files: `Sound.play("hit_blade")`, `"coin_gain"`, `"step_snow"`. Each event in audio.json's `events` names its family and its own takes (`"sfx/hit"`, a rendered WAV; `"blip/tick"`, one of `sound.gd`'s `UI_SOUNDS`); a moment with no sound yet is an event with no takes (silent). A new moment gets its own event, even on a sound it shares today, so its pool can grow alone. `Sound` picks a take at random, never the one it played last, on its own seeded dice; a stinger's variation stays on the game's dice exactly where it was, so flows give the same report with the packs or without. The same take within 40 ms sounds once, whichever moment asked. Tables choose an event: `blows` (a weapon's drawing), `casts` (a skill's theme), `steps` (the tile underfoot; `"over:tile"` first, `over` being the street's paving under the hero or else the map's style, so a cave's `floor` is stone and a house's wood), `swings` (a bow's shot, a staff's bolt), `gathers` (ore struck, herbs pulled), `voices` (a foe's kind: `<kind>_attack`, `_hurt`, `_death`, PIX-413), `Sound.of_family` (`kill_<family>`, `foe_noticed_<family>`), `impact_for` (a skill's theme where it lands). A call naming an event through a table or a format (`"animal_%s"`) is invisible to `test_audio.py`'s scan: add its pattern to that test's `chosen`.
- **Places** (`places`, PIX-413): `Sound.ambience_for(map, region, night, rain, river)` gives a place (`forest_day`, `coast`, `ash`, `frost`, `mines`, `cave`, `marsh_night`, `town`...; PIX-414: `smithy` for the forge, `crypt` for the Greyhold cellars and the Kings' Vault, `ice_cave`, `river` within `RIVER_NEAR` cells of running water on the Reach's open land and in the village (`Sound.near_river`: water or a bridge, not the still pools, lake and ice of `PunyTerrain.STILL_WATERS`; the ash and the marsh keep theirs), and a shower heard as `rain`, `rain_forest` (the Deepwood, the Reach's woods) or `rain_inside` (a village house's roof, `Soundscape.rain_on_roof`)); its `sprinkle` is its one-shots, each a render of the game's or (PIX-414) an event the packs fill, `cave`'s drips and stones (silent without them), thrown on Sound's own dice so a sprinkle never moves the game's, as are the extras (birds, crickets, chatter, fire; PIX-415); its `bed` an `ambience`-family event whose pack take loops under the music in place of the game's wind or silence (`Sound.set_bed(name, hushed)`; the rain always wins, the pack's rain only where a shower is the place, `RAIN_PLACES`; a burning or empty square hushes it). - **Levels**: every file has a dB in audio.json's `levels` (`Sound.level_of`, keyed by its path under `assets/audio/` without a variant's number), applied before the player's buses. The renders came out at whatever loudness their synth gave, so a new one is measured (`ffmpeg -af ebur128=peak=true`: integrated LUFS for music, the 400 ms max for a short effect) and levelled to its family: music at -37.5 LUFS, effects beside the ones played in the same moment, its peak with its level under -12 dBTP (at the default volume the bus adds 10.9 dB, below). `tools/test_audio.py` refuses a file without a level, a `Sound.play`/`play_track`/`play_theme` name that doesn't exist (Sound skips those silently), an event no moment asks for, and any family's loudest take (a pack's at its family's peak) passing 0 dBFS through its bus at the full slider; an installed `takes.json` event that audio.json doesn't have yet it skips with a note (PIX-414: the private repo is pushed before the PR naming its events merges, and other branches' CI installs it).
- **Loudness** (PIX-383): every bus plays `Sound.LOUDNESS_DB` (+14) over the player's slider (`Sound.bus_db`), so at the default 70% the music is about -23.6 LUFS as heard and a busy fight about -19.5, and Master carries a hard limiter at -1 dBFS (`CEILING_DB`) for sounds stacked at once. The web build plays its sounds as samples (the music streams), which run no bus effects: there the limiter does nothing, so keep the headroom that lets a fight all but never pass 0 dBFS without it.
